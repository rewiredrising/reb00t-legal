No Nut November rules: what counts and what doesn't

No one runs No Nut November, so there's no official rulebook. It started as an Urban Dictionary joke in 2011 and went viral on Reddit and Twitter around 2017. What the community has settled on since then comes down to a few levels:

ModeWhat you give upWho it's for
StandardMasturbating to orgasmPeople doing it for the meme
SeriousMasturbation and pornAnyone who wants the month to actually change something
Hard modeMasturbation, porn, edging, and "soft" content like thirst-trap feedsGuys using NNN to reset a habit they're sick of

A few questions come up every year:

  • Wet dreams don't count. They're involuntary. The challenge is about what you do while you're awake.
  • Partnered sex is up to you. The original joke was about going solo, and plenty of people in relationships exclude it. If you want a clean reset, include it.
  • Edging counts as playing with fire. Technically you didn't finish. In practice, edging is how most No Nut November attempts end.

If you're doing NNN because porn has become something you reach for automatically, go with Serious or Hard mode. The Standard version lets you keep the exact habit you're trying to break.

Why most people fail No Nut November in week one

No Nut November searches peak in the first few days of November every year and fall off fast after that. That pattern matches what you'd expect from a habit. The urge usually isn't about desire. It's a cue-and-routine loop that's been running for years.

The usual cues are boring and predictable:

  • Bed plus phone. Late at night, lights off, scrolling. For a lot of guys, this one cue is behind most slips.
  • Boredom and stress. A dead afternoon, a deadline you're dodging, a fight with someone.
  • Being alone with nothing planned, especially on weekend mornings.
  • Alcohol, which quietly removes every rule you made sober.
  • "Just checking" social media, where the algorithm knows exactly what you've been looking at.

Recovery coaches often use the shorthand HALT: hungry, angry, lonely, tired. If you can name which of those you're feeling when an urge shows up, you've already taken some of the autopilot out of it.

Before November 1: lock down your phone

The single highest-leverage thing you can do is decide now, while you're calm, so you don't have to decide at 1 a.m. Spend 20 minutes on this list during the last week of October:

  1. Block adult sites in every browser, not just Safari. On iPhone, Screen Time's "Limit Adult Websites" setting (under Content & Privacy Restrictions) filters Safari and other supported browsers like Chrome. Apple has moved that menu around between iOS versions, so if you can't find it, search "adult websites" in Settings.
  2. Make the blocker hard to undo. A filter you can turn off in ten seconds won't hold up in a weak moment. Have a friend set the Screen Time passcode, or use an app that keeps the blocker on so turning it off takes real effort.
  3. Clean up your feeds. Unfollow the accounts that act as a gateway. If Instagram Explore or TikTok's For You page is a trigger, delete the app for the month. You can reinstall it on December 1.
  4. Charge your phone outside the bedroom. A $10 alarm clock might be the best purchase you make all month.
  5. Write down your reason. Be specific: "I want my focus back," "I want to stop feeling like garbage at 2 a.m.," "I want to be present with her." You'll read it during urges.
  6. Tell one person or join a community that's doing it too. It's a lot harder to quietly break a rule that someone else knows about.

What to do when an urge hits: the 5-minute protocol

Urges feel permanent, but they aren't. Most rise, peak and fade within minutes if you don't feed them. Psychologists call riding one out "urge surfing." You aren't trying to win an argument with your brain. You're trying to get through the next five minutes without opening the door.

This is the sequence we built Quit & REBOOT around, and you can do it with or without the app:

  1. Lock in. Name the real trigger. Out loud or on paper: "I'm bored and avoiding work," "I'm lonely," "I'm in bed with my phone again." Naming it moves you out of autopilot.
  2. Breathe. Five slow cycles. Inhale through your nose, take a second short inhale on top, then let out a long, slow exhale. Long, slow exhales are one of the quickest ways to calm a racing nervous system.
  3. Move. Twenty reps, right now. Jumping jacks, squats or push-ups. Physical exertion forces a real change in your state: heart rate up, attention pulled into your body. It's very hard to keep an urge going in the middle of a set.
  4. Declare. Read your reason. The one you wrote before November 1. Read it in full.
  5. Check in. Rate the urge again. If it was an 8 out of 10 before and it's a 3 now, write that down. Every logged win is proof for next time that urges pass.

The "move" step is the one people skip, and it's the one that works best. In the app, your iPhone camera counts your reps with on-device pose tracking, so you can't wave it off with "I'll do them later." By the time you finish twenty jumping jacks, the urge has usually lost most of its pull.

Your No Nut November plan, week by week

Week 1 (Nov 1–7): break the autopilot

This is the hardest week by volume because every old cue is still active. Expect urges at the same times and places as always. Your only job is to interrupt them. Phone out of the bedroom, protocol on every urge, and plan your first weekend in advance. November 1 is a Sunday, so the month opens on a lazy weekend day. Get out of the house that morning.

Week 2 (Nov 8–14): boredom and bargaining

The novelty is gone. Now the urges get clever: "Just looking isn't breaking the rules." "One quick scroll." "You've earned it." This is where edging and "soft" content show up. Recognize the bargaining for what it is and treat it like any other urge. Fill the dead time with real activity: train, cook, call someone, start the project you keep postponing.

Week 3 (Nov 15–21): watch the overconfidence

You feel good, maybe better than you have in a while, and that's when people loosen the rules. They delete the blocker "because I don't need it anymore," or they stay up scrolling in bed again. Don't. Some guys also hit a flat, low-energy stretch around here. If that happens, don't treat it as a sign something's wrong. Keep the routine going.

Week 4 (Nov 22–30): finish, then decide what's next

Thanksgiving week often brings travel, family stress and broken routines. Pack your plan with you. In the last few days, decide what December looks like. Will you keep the blocker on? Go for 60 or 90 days? You'll make that call far better on day 28 than at midnight on day 30.

10 No Nut November tips that actually help

  1. Sleep without your phone. It's worth repeating.
  2. Train daily, even for 15 minutes. You'll sleep better, feel less restless and have less energy left over for urges.
  3. Get morning light and a fixed wake-up time. Bad sleep makes cravings worse.
  4. Keep a replacement list of five things you can do in under two minutes: a set of push-ups, a walk around the block, a cold splash of water, a text to a friend, your reason card.
  5. Don't keep "just in case" content saved anywhere. Clear bookmarks, saved posts and private folders before November 1.
  6. Treat edging as a relapse, because in practice it nearly always leads to one.
  7. Plan weekends hour by hour for the first two. Unstructured time is the riskiest time.
  8. Limit alcohol, or at least decide your rules before the first drink.
  9. Track your streak where you'll see it. A visible count is easier to protect.
  10. Log every urge, including what time it came and what triggered it. By week three you'll see your patterns clearly enough to get ahead of them.

No Nut November benefits: what the science actually says

You'll see big claims about No Nut November: superpowers, testosterone through the roof, a magnetic aura. Most of them don't hold up, and you should know that going in.

  • Testosterone: The day-7 testosterone spike people quote comes from a 2003 study of 28 men, and that paper was retracted in 2021. There's no solid evidence that a month without ejaculating changes testosterone in any meaningful, lasting way.
  • Sexual health: A 2025 study in The Journal of Sexual Medicine followed No Nut November participants before and after November 2023. It found no real difference in sexual pleasure, desire or dysfunction compared with non-participants. Abstaining didn't make things better or worse.
  • Long-term health: A 2016 study of nearly 32,000 men, published in European Urology, linked more frequent ejaculation with a lower risk of prostate cancer. That's not a reason to skip NNN, but a month off isn't a health hack either.

So why do it at all? Because for a lot of guys the real problem isn't hormones. It's a habit. Compulsive sexual behavior disorder is now recognized by the World Health Organization in ICD-11, and many men who'd never meet that bar still feel porn has more control over their time and attention than they'd like. Thirty days is long enough to see the habit clearly, practice beating urges dozens of times, and find out what your evenings feel like without it. Those are the benefits that last.

What happens if you fail No Nut November?

Nothing, unless you let one slip turn into ten. Researchers call it the abstinence violation effect: you break a rule, decide the whole thing is ruined, and binge because "it doesn't matter anymore." That reaction does far more damage than the slip itself.

If it happens:

  1. Restart your count the same day. Not on Monday, and not on December 1.
  2. Write down the details: time, place, mood, app, what you'd been doing for the hour before.
  3. Close that one gap. If it happened in bed at 1 a.m. with your phone, that's your fix for tonight.
  4. Keep going. One slip in thirty days is still a good month, and much better than quitting on day six.

After November 30: keep the momentum

The month is a starting line, not a finish line. A lot of people are surprised by how much time and headspace comes back in thirty days, and they don't want to give it back on December 1. If that's you, keep the blocker on, keep logging urges, and set a new target like 60 or 90 days. There's nothing magic about those numbers. They just give you enough time for new routines to feel normal.

Whether you're doing No Nut November as a joke with friends or as the start of something more serious, the approach is the same: make slipping harder, have a plan for the next five minutes, and track your progress.
